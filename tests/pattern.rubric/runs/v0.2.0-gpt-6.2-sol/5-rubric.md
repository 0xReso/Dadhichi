Assumed: The reader is the email sender, and the expected output is a ready-to-send revision of their draft.

| # | Check | Pass condition | Decided by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Revised email delivered | The output includes a revised email body that the sender can copy as one block without removing commentary. | grader |
| 2 | Explicit instructions followed | The output satisfies every explicit editing instruction the sender gave. | grader |
| 3 | Source meaning preserved | The revision preserves every material detail and level of certainty in the draft unless the sender authorizes a change. | grader |
| 4 | No invented details | Every new factual claim or commitment in the revision is supported by the sender’s draft or accompanying context. | grader |
| 5 | Purpose and next step | By the end of its first substantive paragraph, the email states why the sender is writing and identifies any response or action they want from the recipient. | grader |
| 6 | Recipient appropriate tone | The wording fits the recipient relationship and situation established by the input. | grader |
| 7 | No redundant sentences | Each sentence contributes a distinct message, transition, or social function. | grader |
| 8 | Correct readable prose | Each sentence is free of errors in spelling, grammar, or punctuation that change its meaning, force a reread, or leave a visible typo. | grader |

## Grading conventions

- **1.** Grade each requested email as a section. One missing or non-copyable body is Fail. A separately labeled subject line or a note outside the email passes. If the sender supplies no draft or message content, a focused request for the missing material passes and checks 2–8 do not apply.
- **2.** Compare the output with the sender’s explicit instructions, one requirement at a time. One unmet requirement is Fail. Treat preferences inferred from the draft under the relevant check below.
- **3.** Compare the draft and revision one meaning-bearing detail at a time, including who does what, dates, amounts, conditions, and whether a statement is tentative or definite. One material omission or alteration is Fail. Rephrasing that preserves the meaning passes. Score changes to existing details here and additions of distinct details under check 4.
- **4.** Compare each added factual claim or commitment with the draft and accompanying context. One unsupported addition is Fail. Greetings, thanks, and clearly marked placeholders pass unless they assert an unsupported fact or promise.
- **5.** Grade the first substantive paragraph, skipping the salutation and any standalone courtesy line. One missing purpose or delayed request is Fail. A straightforward statement of purpose passes for an informational email with no requested action. Score an omitted source request under check 3.
- **6.** Compare each passage with the relationship and situation stated or evident in the input. One clear mismatch is Fail, such as a joke in a condolence note or blame in a neutral follow-up. If the relationship is unspecified, courteous, direct language passes. Score violations of explicit tone instructions under check 2.
- **7.** Grade one sentence at a time. One sentence that repeats an earlier point without adding emphasis needed for the message, context, a transition, or a distinct social function is Fail. A brief thanks or a closing reminder that ties a deadline to the requested action passes.
- **8.** Grade each sentence and standalone text line, including the subject if present. One qualifying error is Fail. Intentional fragments, contractions, and consistent regional spelling pass. Score an error that changes a material source detail under check 3.
