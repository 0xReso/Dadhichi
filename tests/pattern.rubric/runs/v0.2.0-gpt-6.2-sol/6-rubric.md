## Grading conventions

- **1 — Take home schedule.** Score the response as a whole. One usable patient-facing instruction or, when no dose is resolvable, one direction to an appropriate contact passes this check. A response consisting only of drug facts or a description of how to write a schedule fails. Score missing individual steps under check 3 or 4.
- **2 — Accurate schedule details.** Compare each stated instruction with the discharge information supplied in the test input. One wrong or unsupported instruction fails, including a guessed dose, a clock time asserted without support, or missed-dose advice absent from the source. An equivalent restatement passes. Score an omitted confirmed instruction under check 3 and a silent unresolved detail under check 4.
- **3 — Complete dosing steps.** Compare each confirmed instruction in the input with the output. One omission fails if it changes what the patient needs to do, including a supplied medication name or required strength, amount and unit, route, timing or frequency, start time, dose change, stop point, or condition for taking or holding a dose. A detail absent from the input is outside this check. If the entire response fails check 1 because it gives no patient directions, skip this check.
- **4 — Unresolved dosing details.** Apply this check only when the input leaves an action necessary to follow the schedule unresolved. One such detail left silent, or flagged without a contact and an instruction to confirm before acting on the affected part, fails. Explicitly deferring that part while giving confirmed instructions passes. Score a guessed resolution under check 2 and skip this check for that detail.
- **5 — Dose and time pairing.** Apply this check to each stated dose for which the output also states the timing or trigger. One pair split across sentences, list items, or table rows fails. A shared table header counts as part of each row, and one sentence can pair a dose with several times. Score absent confirmed amounts or times under check 3.
- **6 — Plain dosing language.** Score each first use of a term the patient must understand to act. One unexplained term such as “BID” or “PRN” fails. A translation beside the term passes, and medication names and standard units such as “mg” need no explanation. Score an incorrect translation under check 2.
- **7 — Focused content.** Score paragraphs, list items, and table rows. One entirely unrelated unit or two standalone sentences that add nothing to an otherwise relevant unit fail. A brief greeting or one redundant sentence passes, as does a source-provided instruction about food, missed doses, or when to seek help. Score unsupported dosing instructions under check 2.
