## Grading conventions

1. **Article word limit.** Count whitespace-separated tokens containing a letter or digit in the rendered article, including the title, headings, outcome line, and closing material. Fix the topic size from the requested mechanism before grading any outputs for that input. A simple mechanism has one object or value with one job, a moderate mechanism has several dependent steps, and a deep mechanism requires several stages with distinct foundations. Use an explicit length in the reader notes as the cap when supplied. If the notes say the reader already knows a foundation, use the corresponding cap of 450, 1,500, or 3,500 words. One word above the applicable cap is Fail. Grade the body’s use of space separately under check 6.

2. **Article structure.** Count Markdown blocks using the size fixed for check 1. Require a title followed by one standalone prose line and a body. A simple body has no section headings. A moderate body has three to five `##` body sections. A deep body has multiple `##` chapters. Moderate and deep bodies end with `## The whole picture` containing one paragraph. All sizes may add a final `## Going further` with two to four entries. One missing required block, extra section, or misplaced closing section is Fail. An unheaded paragraph break in a simple body passes.

3. **Requested concept explained.** Before comparing outputs, use the topic and any supplied material to name the central mechanism steps and one consequential misunderstanding. Mark Fail if any of the four answers is absent or a related concept substitutes for the requested one. An implicit answer passes if a grader can quote the passage that supplies it. For the prompt’s named-tool fallback, the opening line must state the lack of reliable knowledge, the body must explain the general kind of tool, and the ending must give three or four documentation questions. Score factual mistakes under check 4 instead of treating them as omissions here.

4. **Claims and confidence.** Grade individual claims against the supplied material when present and against established technical knowledge otherwise. One contradiction, unsupported topic-specific assertion, invented origin or attribution, overstatement of certainty or scope, or inference presented as a reported fact is Fail, including in the closing synthesis. A caveat at the first change in confidence can cover the passage it clearly governs. General practice marked as such and a connection explicitly marked as the author’s reading pass. A claim of unknown or unpublished status requires support from the supplied material. Silence supports only a statement that the sources behind the article do not cover the point.

5. **First use readiness.** Grade each sentence at its position in the article. One necessary technical term, acronym, or dependency left unexplained at first use is Fail. The topic name in the title and ordinary technology vocabulary the reader already knows pass. A plain-language explanation in the same sentence passes. An explicit teaser with no missing prerequisite is scored under check 8 only.

6. **Body scope.** Grade each removable sentence or paragraph by asking which required explanation, worked step, or misconception correction would become incomplete if it were deleted. One substantive aside with no such effect is Fail. Apply this to configuration keys, version details, implementation variants, optional strategies such as alternative read paths or pre-vote, history, and extra worked cases. A brief qualifier passes if removing it would make a core claim inaccurate. Score recap placement under check 8 and example consistency under check 7.

7. **Worked example continuity.** Follow the case by its actors or objects, inputs, actions, and result. One missing worked case, unexplained change of case, or inconsistent value is Fail. A case spanning only a few sentences passes for a single exchange. The named-tool fallback may illustrate general practice without assigning the example to the tool.

8. **Section handoffs.** Grade each body section’s opening and ending, and each sentence that explicitly defers an explanation. In a sectioned article, each body section after the first opens with one or two sentences of re-entry. One missing or longer re-entry, an end-of-section recap, or a forward reference in the body is Fail. A single final question that the next body section actually answers passes if the current section already makes sense without the answer. Simple articles have no re-entry requirement.

9. **Standalone reading.** Grade each presentation element. One hyperlink in the article, diagram, table, interactive element, instruction to consult an outside resource during the read, or code block on a topic that is not itself about code is Fail. A short flat list of sequential steps, a few short lines of code explained immediately on a code topic, and plain-text terms for later lookup pass.
