How bond prices react to interest rates — Medium depth (60 questions)
Assumptions: Unless a question states otherwise, every bond has a $100 face value (the amount repaid at maturity), pays fixed annual coupons at year-end, has no default risk, cannot be repaid early (non-callable), and is priced on a coupon date (no accrued interest) using annual compounding with yields quoted as effective annual rates on a flat yield curve.

## Band 1: Isolating the Core Mechanisms

**Q1.** You buy a 5-year fixed-rate bond with a $100 face value and a 4% annual coupon (interest payment). The next day, market interest rates jump from 4% to 7% and stay at 7%. How many dollars does the issuer pay you at the end of Year 1?

- (a) It rises above $100
- (b) It stays at $100
- (c) It falls below $100
- (d) It cannot be sold until Year 5

**Q3.** A 1-year bond pays no coupon and repays its $100 face value one year from today. The market interest rate for 1-year bonds is 10%. What is the bond's price today?

- (b) $90.91
- (c) $100.00
- (d) $110.00

**Q4.** Bond A pays a 6% annual coupon and matures in 5 years. Bond B pays a 3% annual coupon and matures in 5 years. Today's market yield (interest rate) for 5-year bonds is 4%. How do their market prices compare to their $100 face values?

- (b) Bond A trades above $100; Bond B trades below $100
- (c) Bond A trades below $100; Bond B trades above $100

**Q5.** A 2-year bond with a $100 face value pays a $4 annual coupon and sells today for $96. An investor calculates `$4 / $96 = 4.17%` and claims that is their annual return if they hold the bond to maturity. How does their true annual return (yield to maturity) compare to 4.17%?

- (b) Lower than 4.17%, because the bond sells below face value
- (c) Exactly 4.00%, matching the coupon rate
- (d) Higher than 4.17%, because they also earn a $4 price gain by maturity

**Q6.** Today you buy a 3-year bond with a 2% annual coupon when the market yield is 6%, paying $89.31. Over the next year, market interest rates stay locked at 6% without moving at all. Right after the Year 1 coupon is paid (with 2 years left), what is true about the bond's market price?

**Q7.** Bond A matures in 2 years and Bond B matures in 15 years. Both pay a 5% annual coupon and trade at $100 today (market yield 5%). Tomorrow, market yields rise to 6% for all maturities. Which bond loses a larger percentage of its price?

**Q10.** Consider a 10-year zero-coupon bond (pays no coupons; repays $100 at Year 10). In Scenario 1, market yields rise from 2% to 3%. In Scenario 2, market yields rise from 10% to 11%. In which scenario does the bond lose a larger percentage of its starting price?
